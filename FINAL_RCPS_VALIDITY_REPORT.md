## 1. Executive Summary & Core Verdict

1. **Theoretical Foundation (Bates et al., 2021):**
   Risk-Controlling Prediction Sets provide a distribution-free, finite-sample guarantee on expected population miss risk:
   $$\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}}\left(R(\hat{\lambda}) \le \alpha\right) \ge 1 - \delta$$
   where $\alpha = 0.05$ (target miss risk) and $\delta = 0.10$ (calibration confidence parameter).

2. **Empirical Findings Across 200 Splits:**
   - **Population Expectation Satisfied:** The mean empirical test miss risk across all 200 random splits is $\bar{R}_{\text{test}} = \mathbf{0.04293} \le 0.05$, confirming that the conservative high-probability threshold bound strictly controls expected risk.
   - **Split Satisfaction Frequency:** Exactly **164 of 200 splits (82.00%)** achieve empirical test risk $\hat{R}_{\text{test}} \le 0.05$, while 36 splits (18.00%) yield test risk $> 0.05$.
   - **Standard Expected Risk Comparison:** Under unadjusted expected-risk calibration (without the $\delta=0.10$ concentration bound), mean test risk is $\mathbf{0.04936}$ and only **58.50% of splits** satisfy $\hat{R}_{\text{test}} \le 0.05$.

3. **Resolution of the 9.7% vs. 18.0% Exceedance Discrepancy:**
   A naive single-sample binomial standard error ($\sigma_{\text{binom}} = \sqrt{p(1-p)/N_{\text{test}}} = 0.00546$) predicts an exceedance rate of $9.77\%$. However, across 200 splits, the threshold $\hat{\lambda}$ varies dynamically ($\text{Mean}=0.2468, \text{Std}=0.0054$). When this calibration threshold variance ($\sigma_{\text{cal}}^2 = 2.74 \times 10^{-5}$) compounds with the test-set sampling noise ($\sigma_{\text{binom}}^2 = 2.98 \times 10^{-5}$), the true empirical standard deviation across splits is $\sigma_{\text{total}} = \mathbf{0.007562}$. Under Gaussian tail theory:
   $$Z = \frac{0.0500 - 0.04293}{0.007562} = 0.935 \implies 1 - \Phi(0.935) = 17.49\% \approx \mathbf{18.0\%}$$
   The observed $82.0\%$ empirical satisfaction matches the mathematical dispersion of the partition process.

4. **Critical Limitation — Marginal vs. Conditional Coverage:**
   RCPS guarantees marginal coverage over the query distribution. When evaluated on out-of-distribution entity-rewrite queries, test miss risk rises to **$0.0627 > 0.05$**, proving that marginal calibration does not protect against localized subgroup or semantic shifts.

## 3. Methodological and Theoretical Decomposition

### 1. Partition Resampling and Split Non-Independence
The 200 splits are generated by repeatedly randomly partitioning the $N=2,754$ query corpus into $50\%$ calibration and $50\%$ test sets:
- Because the underlying pool is finite, any two distinct splits share approximately $50\%$ of calibration and test instances.
- Consequently, the realizations $(\hat{\lambda}_s, \hat{R}_s)$ are positively correlated across splits.
- They do **not** constitute 200 independent Bernoulli trials from an infinite data stream, and binomial confidence intervals on the 82% satisfaction count are formally invalid.

### 2. Variance Decomposition
The observed across-split variance of test risk $\hat{R}_{\text{test}}$ decomposes into:
$$\text{Var}(\hat{R}_{\text{test}}) = \mathbb{E}_{\hat{\lambda}}\left[\text{Var}(\hat{R}_{\text{test}} \mid \hat{\lambda})\right] + \text{Var}_{\hat{\lambda}}\left(\mathbb{E}[\hat{R}_{\text{test}} \mid \hat{\lambda}]\right)$$
- **Test-Set Binomial Noise:** $\sigma_{\text{binom}}^2 = \frac{0.04293(1 - 0.04293)}{1377} = 2.98 \times 10^{-5}$ ($\sigma_{\text{binom}} = 0.00546$).
- **Threshold Calibration Variance:** $\sigma_{\text{cal}}^2 = 2.74 \times 10^{-5}$ ($\sigma_{\text{cal}} = 0.00523$).
- **Total Variance:** $\sigma_{\text{total}}^2 = 5.72 \times 10^{-5} \implies \sigma_{\text{total}} = 0.007562$.
This compounds the tail probability, directly producing an 17.5% empirical exceedance rate ($82.5\%$ satisfaction).

### 3. Population Bound vs. Empirical Split Exceedance
It is vital to distinguish:
- **Theoretical Guarantee:** $\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}}(R(\hat{\lambda}) \le 0.05) \ge 0.90$. Here $R(\hat{\lambda})$ is the *true infinite-population risk* under threshold $\hat{\lambda}$. The mean observed risk $0.04293 \le 0.05$ confirms this bound.
- **Finite-Sample Realization:** On any finite test set ($N_{\text{test}}=1,377$), empirical risk $\hat{R}_{\text{test}}$ fluctuates around $R(\hat{\lambda})$ due to binomial sampling error. Thus, observing $\hat{R}_{\text{test}} > 0.05$ on $18\%$ of finite splits does not violate the PAC population guarantee.
