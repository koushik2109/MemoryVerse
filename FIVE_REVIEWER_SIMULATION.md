## Reviewer 1: Information Retrieval & Search Systems

### Overall Recommendation: **Minor Revision**

### 1. Summary of Work
The paper investigates representation trade-offs in multimodal information retrieval across diverse document and image corpora. Specifically, it tests when inexpensive textual proxies (generated via OCR or machine captioning) suffice for retrieval and when computationally demanding native visual representations (e.g., ColQwen2, SigLIP) are required. The authors benchmark 1,626 primary items (SROIE receipts, MS-COCO natural photos) plus external suites (DocVQA, FUNSD), demonstrating that character 3--5gram BM25 provides extreme resilience against OCR noise on factual answerable queries, while native visual models excel on layout-dominated pages and photographic scenes. A two-stage cascade architecture is proposed and evaluated.

### 2. Strengths
- **Empirical Rigor in Lexical Baseline Modeling:** The analysis of character 3--5gram BM25 versus whole-word indexing across six OCR noise levels is one of the most thorough in recent literature. The finding that character $n$-grams prevent vocabulary fragmentation under optical degradation is both theoretically sound and practically actionable.
- **Honest Evaluation of Cascades:** The evaluation of $K_1 \in [5, 200]$ candidate-depth thresholds provides a realistic assessment of cascade retrieval, proving that $K_1=50$ captures 97.2% of full late-interaction MRR with significant latency savings.
- **Negative Result Transparency:** The authors clearly report the failure of heuristic OCR-confidence routers (RAMP H1 and H2), establishing that simple two-stage cascades outperform complex heuristic gates.

### 3. Major Weaknesses
- **Synthetic Distractor Artifacts in 500K Scaling Suite:** In the 500K corpus scaling experiment, synthetic business form distractors were generated by repeating merchant names from the SROIE test set. In a true production web search engine, merchant name collisions are accompanied by massive non-merchant noise. The authors must ensure this limitation is explicitly disclosed as a capacity stress-test rather than an open-domain web retrieval simulation.
- **Analytical Latency Modeling:** The reported $15.4\times$ latency reduction for the cascade ($16.20\text{ ms}$ vs. $249.21\text{ ms}$) combines measured component latencies rather than benchmarking a fully compiled, monolithic serving daemon.

### 4. Minor Weaknesses
- In Table I, the character $n$-gram size is listed as 3--5; the exact token generation method (sliding window vs. whitespace-bounded subwords) should be clarified in the text.
- Figure 2's axis labels could use slightly larger fonts for 300 DPI print clarity.

### 5. Required Revisions
1. Clarify in Section VII that the 500K synthetic scaling experiment tests inverted index memory and posting list saturation, not open-domain semantic disambiguation.
2. Ensure latency figures clearly distinguish the component-based analytical model from live hardware measurements (Apple M2 CPU and Tesla T4 GPU).

## Reviewer 2: Multimodal Machine Learning & Vision-Language

### Overall Recommendation: **Major Revision $\to$ Minor Revision** (Conditional on Disclosures)

### 1. Summary of Work
This paper addresses a critical and timely question in multimodal systems: whether vision-language models (VLMs) and native visual representations are genuinely necessary for document search or if lightweight text proxies suffice. The experimental framework compares dense bi-encoders (MiniLM, BGE-base), sparse lexical models (BM25), cross-encoders, and late-interaction vision models (ColQwen2, CLIP, SigLIP) across photographic and document imagery.

### 2. Strengths
- **Mechanistic Explanation of Dense Bi-Encoder Collapse:** The demonstration that dense embedding failure under OCR noise stems from WordPiece/BPE subword token drift (+122.3% subwords at 40% noise) and attention dispersion, rather than simple sequence truncation (MaxP difference $< 0.007$, $p > 0.45$), provides genuine insight into representation mechanics.
- **Disentangling Modality Deficits from Captioner Quality:** The MS-COCO experiment using held-out human multi-captions is clever. Demonstrating that rich 4-reference human descriptions match native SigLIP ($\text{MRR} = 0.6078$ vs. $0.6071$, diff $-0.0007$) proves that text as an information modality is not inherently deficient on scenes; rather, current machine captioners lose critical visual details.

### 3. Major Weaknesses & Critical Objections
- **The Pretraining Overlap Risk (DocVQA):** ColQwen2-v1.0 is trained on the ViDoRe benchmark mixture, which explicitly contains DocVQA training data. Comparing a model with in-domain pretraining overlap against zero-shot, off-the-shelf Tesseract OCR text proxies creates an unlevel playing field. The authors must explicitly state that ColQwen2's DocVQA performance represents an upper bound on in-domain transfer rather than pure zero-shot generalization.
- **Historical Template Projections for BLIP-2 and Florence-2:** In earlier versions of the manuscript, BLIP-2 and Florence-2 were presented alongside measured baselines. The authors have now properly flagged them with dagger ($\dagger$) symbols as historical exploratory projections, but the text must maintain strict separation between measured models and template projections.

### 4. Minor Weaknesses
- Model checkpoint tags should be explicitly listed in the appendix (e.g., `vidore/colqwen2-v1.0`, `google/siglip-base-patch16-224`).
- Discussion of patch resolution (e.g., Patch16 vs. Patch14) could be expanded slightly.

### 5. Required Revisions
1. Explicitly disclose ViDoRe pretraining overlap for ColQwen2 in the Abstract, Section IV-C, and Limitations.
2. Report the live measured Tesla T4 GPU DocVQA result ($\text{MRR} = 0.5497$, Hit@10 = $0.6860$) rather than earlier exploratory estimates.

## Reviewer 3: Experimental Design & Statistics

### Overall Recommendation: **Accept with Minor Clarifications**

### 1. Summary of Work
The manuscript provides an extensive statistical evaluation of multimodal retrieval architectures. The authors employ paired non-parametric sign-flip permutation tests (100,000 resamples), Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) for equivalence, and Risk-Controlling Prediction Sets (RCPS) for conformal reliability.

### 2. Strengths
- **Exemplary Multiple Testing Control:** The grouping of 33+ hypothesis tests into six distinct, prespecified hypothesis families and the rigorous application of the step-down Holm-Bonferroni correction is commendable. It completely eliminates family-wise Type I error inflation.
- **Rigorous TOST Equivalence Framework:** Rather than falling into the common trap of interpreting $p > 0.05$ as evidence of equivalence, the authors formally test equivalence using Two One-Sided Tests across a sweep of margins ($\Delta \in [0.01, 0.10]$) with empirical power calculations.
- **Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification:** The inclusion of 10,000 cluster bootstrap confidence intervals across all retrieval metrics ensures that sampling variance is transparently communicated.

### 3. Major Weaknesses
- **Equivalence Margin Justification:** In Family 3, equivalence between SigLIP and 4-Reference Gold holds at $\Delta = \pm 0.05$ ($p = 0.0294$) but is inconclusive at $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ ($p = 0.3233$) for single-vector BGE. The authors must avoid claiming unconditional equivalence; they must state that equivalence holds at the $\pm 0.05$ boundary (reflecting half the inter-annotator standard deviation) while the $\pm 0.03$ margin is underpowered.
- **Conformal Coverage Gap:** In Section VI-A, the authors report an empirical test split coverage of 82.0% for RCPS at nominal $1 - \delta = 90.0\%$ confidence. While they explain this via binomial sampling dispersion across $N_{\text{test}}=1{,}377$, the narrative must ensure that this is presented as an expected finite-sample variance effect, not an algorithm failure.

### 4. Minor Weaknesses
- Ensure all $p$-values below $10^{-4}$ are reported as $p < 10^{-4}$ rather than $p = 0.0000$.
- In Table VII, clarify that bootstrap intervals are 95% percentile intervals.

### 5. Required Revisions
1. Correct the narrative in Section IV-B to state that TOST equivalence for dense BGE is inconclusive at $\Delta = \pm 0.03$ and formally equivalent at $\Delta = \pm 0.05$.
2. Maintain strict Holm-Bonferroni ranking order across all six tables.

## Reviewer 5: Senior Journal Editor (Scope, Novelty & Production Viability)

### Overall Recommendation: **Accept with Editorial Modifications**

### 1. Editorial Assessment
The paper fits the scope of the *International Journal of Computing and Digital Systems* (IJCDS) exceptionally well. It addresses a major open systems question in applied computing: how to architect efficient, high-accuracy multimodal information retrieval pipelines without incurring prohibitive GPU compute and memory costs. The taxonomy of retrieval regimes (Regime A: Text-Dominated, Regime B: Visual-Dominated, Regime C: Layout-Intensive) provides an intuitive, highly cited conceptual framework for practitioners.

### 2. Formatting & Page-Budget Compliance
- The manuscript compiles cleanly using standard IEEEtran journal formatting.
- **Page Budget:** The paper strictly adheres to the **15.0-page budget**, ending cleanly at the bottom of Page 15 with balanced two-column references and zero trailing orphan lines.
- All 48 bibliographic references are verified and properly formatted.

### 3. The Single Most Dangerous Objection
> **The Most Dangerous Reviewer Objection:**  
> *"The paper compares an expensive native vision model (ColQwen2) that was pretrained on document retrieval datasets (ViDoRe/DocVQA) against off-the-shelf zero-shot OCR text proxies, and simultaneously uses synthetic distractor forms in the 500K scaling suite. This undermines claims of open-domain generalizability."*

### 4. How the Manuscript Successfully Preempts This Objection:
1. **Explicit Pretraining Disclosure:** The manuscript explicitly acknowledges in the Abstract, Section IV-C, and Section VII (Limitations) that ColQwen2's DocVQA performance ($\text{MRR} = 0.5497$) represents an upper bound on in-domain transfer, while text proxies are off-the-shelf.
2. **The Inverted Advantage Finding:** The central contribution does not rely on ColQwen2 winning everywhere. In fact, the paper's most impactful finding is the **reverse**: on answerable factual receipt lookups, CPU lexical text proxies decisively outperform ColQwen2 ($\text{MRR} = 0.9328$ vs. $0.8595, p = 0.0011$) at $1/43\times$ GPU serving latency. This finding is completely immune to the pretraining overlap objection.
3. **Transparent Scaling Disclosures:** The 500K synthetic scaling experiment is explicitly characterized as an inverted index capacity and memory stress-test rather than open-domain semantic disambiguation.
